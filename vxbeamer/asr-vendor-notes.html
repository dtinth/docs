ASR model observations¶

Raw results from running models against test-audio.bin. Observations only — what was sent, what came back. No rankings.

Everything here was run against live vendor endpoints on 2026-07-16.

Fixture¶

test-audio.bin — raw PCM, 16 kHz / 16-bit / mono, little-endian, no header. 294,986 bytes = 9.218 s. Thai speech containing English technical terms (“TypeScript”, “Elysia”, “Railway”, “MongoDB Atlas”).

Method¶

  • Audio sent at realtime pacing: 3200-byte chunks (100 ms), 100 ms apart, unless a row says otherwise.

  • One run per row unless a repeat count is given. These models are LLMs with audio intake and are not deterministic — see Repeat runs, where identical inputs produced different outputs.

  • Transcripts are reproduced exactly, including case, spacing and trailing punctuation.

qwen3.8-omni-flash-realtime (2026-11-12)¶

Served only from a workspace endpoint: wss://<WorkspaceId>.ap-southeast-1.maas.aliyuncs.com/api-ws/v1/realtime?model=qwen3.8-omni-flash-realtime, not the shared dashscope-intl one. No dated snapshot is listed in the vendor’s docs. The vendor’s page describes a new nested session.update shape (audio.input.format, audio.output), but our existing flat handshake (input_audio_format: "pcm", sample_rate, turn_detection: null) is accepted unchanged, so the same adapter drives it.

On the 9.2 s fixture, four realtime runs: two returned the same transcript as qwen3.5-omni-flash-realtime-2026-03-15 (เฟรมเวิร์ก in Thai, deploy in Latin); one rendered deploy as ดีพลอย; one kept framework in Latin. Same token counts (70 audio, 97 text, 29-33 output), about 10 s wall clock, but fewer and coarser partials (9-11 against 31). Four runs is a sample, not a measurement.

Price, from the vendor’s model-pricing page (Singapore, USD per 1M tokens, read 2026-11-12): input text $0.23, input audio $0.93, output text $0.70, output audio $1.87. We ask for text-only output, so the text rate applies: about $0.00011 for this clip. The page lists qwen3.5-omni-flash-realtime at $0.55 / $4.50 / $3.30 / $17.70 on the same four columns. QWEN_OMNI_PRICING used to charge the output audio column ($17.70, and $62 for plus) for text output; it now charges the text column ($3.30 and $12.40), so the 3.5 clip cost is now about $0.00047 rather than $0.00094 (4.4x the 3.8 price, not 8.5x).

qwen-audio-3.1-asr-flash-message and -streaming (2026-11-12)¶

A third Qwen wire protocol: DashScope’s duplex run-task (a run-task text frame, raw PCM as binary frames, finish-task), against wss://<WorkspaceId>.ap-southeast-1.maas.aliyuncs.com/api-ws/v1/inference. Audio may only be sent after task-started. Results come as result-generated with a sentence (sentence_id, sentence_end, text) and cumulative usage tokens.

When partials arrive (9.2 s clip, real-time pacing, last audio chunk at 9.45 s): the Omni models send no partial before the last chunk, because the adapter sends response.create only at the end. Both Audio-3.1 models stream while the audio is still being sent: -streaming 13 partials from 1.3 s, -message 22 partials from 0.5 s (with intermediate_result_enabled; its default is final-only). Every model finishes 0.2-0.7 s after the last chunk.

Partials are not the transcript. -message guesses the language from the first fraction of a second, so its partials are English and Chinese junk until late in the clip (language_hints: ["th"] did not change the partials). -streaming partials are garbled early and readable from about 5 s. The partials only tell the user they are being heard.

Final text, 7 runs of -message paced at real time: 5 gave the good transcript (Thai script, spaced, TypeScript/Elysia/deploy/Railway/MongoDB Atlas in Latin, matching qwen3.8-omni-flash-realtime’s good runs); 2 wrote phonetic Thai for every English word (ไทป์สคริปต์, เอลิเซีย, มองโกดีบีแอดลาสต์) with 77 output tokens instead of 54. 5 runs of -streaming: always no spaces between words, always Latin project/typescript/framework/elysia/deploy/railway in lowercase, and MongoDB Atlas wrong in a different way each time (mongodbb atlas, mongodbbas, mongodbbasas). Neither run-to-run variation is a measurement; n is small.

Faster than real time (the CLI’s --fast, every chunk sent back to back; 3 runs each, 9.2 s clip): all three new models accept it. qwen3.8-omni-flash-realtime 1.07-1.15 s, -message 0.83-0.86 s, -streaming 1.98-2.58 s. The text and token counts are the same as when paced at real time (3.8 and -message the good transcript all 3 times, though the phonetic-Thai variant of -message is seen only when paced, so 3 fast runs do not rule it out; -streaming the same unspaced text with mongodbbas all 3 times), so nothing was dropped. One short clip, so a clip of several minutes sent as fast could still behave differently; not tested.

Tokens and price. -message 116 in / 54 out, -streaming 109 in / 45 out, the vendor’s measured counts. Price, from the vendor’s pricing page (Singapore, per 1M tokens): $0.93 input / $0.70 output for both, about $0.00015 and $0.00013 per clip.

Tuning the message model’s parameters (2026-11-12)¶

qwen-audio-3.1-asr-flash-message takes these parameters on run-task (vendor’s client-events page): disfluency_removal_enabled (its “native text polishing”: drops fillers), language_hints (up to 4 codes; applies to finals), vocabulary / vocabulary_id (hotwords, weight 1-5, or 50 for a “super” hotword, at most 50 of those), input.context (up to 5 user/assistant turns, 400 characters a turn), max_sentence_silence (200-6000 ms, default 800), vad_model, speech_noise_threshold, heartbeat, keep_dialect (Chinese dialects) and intermediate_result_enabled. Not tried, for want of audio: vad_model, speech_noise_threshold, keep_dialect.

What was run: the 9.2 s Thai fixture (about 400 sessions in all, 6-56 runs per setting, several sessions in parallel at real-time pacing), and two synthetic English clips made with ffmpeg’s flite voice, one of spoken numbers and one full of “um/uh/you know”. Counts below are runs of the same audio, not independent speakers.

English words in Thai, spelled in Thai script (“phonetic”: ไทป์สคริปต์, เอลิเซีย) as a share of runs on the Thai clip: no parameters 15 of 40; language_hints: ["th"] 1 of 24; disfluency_removal_enabled 0 of 24; hotwords at weight 50 0 of 24 (at weight 5, 5 of 8); a Thai+Latin example turn in context 0 of 24 (a bare word list, 4 of 8); th + polishing 0 of 12, with weight-50 hotwords also 0 of 12. Setting max_sentence_silence at all (200, 800 or 3000) made it worse, 44 of 56; cause unknown, so just do not set it.

Spoken numbers (English clip): with no parameters the model wrote digits in 5 of 6 runs (23 apples, 0812345678, 2026). Every language_hints form tried (en, th, th,en, en,th) wrote words in 24 of 24 (twenty three). Polishing alone gave digits in 18 of 18, and a hint plus polishing gave digits in 30 of 30. So a hint on its own breaks numbers, and polishing repairs it. Thai spoken numbers were not tested: there was no Thai audio with numbers.

Fillers (English clip): off, every um, uh, you know and like is kept; on, all are removed (6 of 6), and Well and So stay.

The streaming model accepts it too, and benefits (4 runs each): on the Thai clip the text gains word spaces, a capital Project and a correct MongoDB Atlas, where without it the text is unspaced and mongodbbas, though ชื่อ loses its last letter in 4 of 4; on the filler clip the fillers are removed 4 of 4.

Conclusion: polishing is the one setting that helped on every axis, so qwen-audio turns it on by default (disfluencyRemoval: false to turn it off).

Grok Voice Transcribe 2.0 (2026-11-12)¶

xAI’s streaming speech-to-text, wss://api.x.ai/v1/stt?model=grok-voice-transcribe-2.0. Everything is configured in the query string; the server sends transcript.created (audio may only follow it), the client sends raw PCM frames, then {"type":"audio.done"}. Priced per second of audio, $0.20 per hour streaming ($0.10 per hour batch), per the vendor’s announcement of 2026-09-18; OpenRouter’s x-ai/grok-stt-2.0 lists the batch rate, $0.000028 per second.

On the 9.2 s Thai fixture (about 50 sessions, some in parallel):

  • Text is the best seen so far on this fixture: Thai in Thai script, every English word in Latin (TypeScript, deploy, Railway, MongoDB Atlas), with word spaces where a Latin word meets Thai. The one variable word is the framework name: Elysia (right) or Elixir, changing from run to run in every setting (Elixir more often; Elysia in 3 of 3 with en + format, too few runs to call that an effect). filler_words is off by default, so fillers are removed.

  • Partials (with interim_results=true) start 1-2 s in and read well, because each is the running text of the chunk being heard. A chunk final (is_final) locks about 3-4 s of speech; an utterance final (speech_final) carries the whole stitched utterance. Without interim_results the first text comes at about 5.5 s.

  • transcript.done.text is empty. Only the utterance finals carry the text, so the adapter builds its final from those (and falls back to the chunk finals plus the last interim if the audio ended without one).

  • Fast dump is no faster: the whole 9.2 s clip sent at once still finished at about 13.5 s, so the provider is declared real-time only.

  • End-of-audio wait is erratic. From audio.done to transcript.done: 0.3-1 s on most single sessions, but 11 s, 18 s, 40 s and 43 s on others, and one session never finished. It happened with interim_results on and off (1 in 4 off), and with the audio sent evenly or with an 800 ms burst, so it is the vendor, not those settings. Several sessions at once made it worse. Worth watching before relying on this as a default; the backend’s own timeouts are what catch a session that never ends.

  • Text formatting (format=true) needs a language and, per the vendor, works for Arabic, Chinese, English, French, German, Japanese, Portuguese, Russian, Spanish, Swedish and Vietnamese: not Thai. Measured on the English numbers clip (2 runs each): no language, th, and th + format all spell the numbers out (twenty three, zero eight one two ...); en + format writes 23, 081-234-5678 and 2026, but also mangled the price (four hundred fifty BAHT became 400 50bht). On the Thai fixture en + format left the text intact in 3 of 3 runs. Thai spoken numbers under an English bias were not tested: no Thai audio with numbers.

Scribe Realtime v2 (2026-11-14)¶

ElevenLabs’ scribe_v2_realtime, wss://api.elevenlabs.io/v1/speech-to-text/realtime, header xi-api-key. $0.22 per hour (the same as scribe_v3_live), per the vendor’s pricing page. The server speaks first (session_started); audio is JSON (input_audio_chunk, base64, commit, sample_rate); the turn ends with an empty chunk and commit: true.

On the 9.2 s Thai fixture (about 30 sessions):

  • Text is the same in every run: Thai in Thai script, Project, TypeScript, framework, Elysia, deploy, Railway, MongoDB Atlas in Latin (5 of 5 at live pace, 5 of 5 fast, 5 of 5 with language_code=th). Elysia right in all of them.

  • Partials: none in the first 2 s (the vendor says processing starts after the first 2 s of audio), the first at about 2.2 s after audio starts, then one a second. Each replaces the one before. They are shaky: the first two are guesses (Project นี้คืออะไรครับ?) and one went to Project ниии-- project before recovering.

  • Commit to final: 0.25-0.31 s at live pace in 5 of 5 sessions.

  • Faster than real time works, and gives the same text. The 9.2 s clip sent all at once finished in 1.5-1.8 s (5 sessions); a 28.7 s clip in 2.5 s; a 37.3 s clip of different speech in 2.7 s. The one difference seen is the Thai spelling of framework in the 28 s clip (เฟรมเวิร์ก fast, framework live).

  • A commit with under 0.3 s of uncommitted audio is refused: commit_throttled, then the socket closes. A 0.1 s clip hits it; the provider pads with silence.

  • Commits sent in quick succession are throttled too: a commit every 9.2 s of audio, with the audio sent all at once, got commit_throttled.

  • The vendor commits by itself after about 36 s of audio. Sent faster than real time, that segment can arrive after our own commit was sent, and nothing on the wire says which committed segment answers our commit, so for a recording over 30 s the provider waits for segments to stop arriving (1 s). Checked live on the 37 s clip.

  • A clip made of the same sentence four times lost most of its text, at live pace and fast (Project นี้เขียนด้วย | มือ โอเค); a 37 s clip of different speech came through whole. So it looks like handling of repeated audio, not the 36 s limit, but the cause is not known.

  • no_verbatim=true removed um/uh (2 of 2, synthetic English), and wrote the phone number as 0812345678 and the year as 2026 in 1 of 2 runs (the other spelled them out). Default is verbatim: fillers kept, numbers as words, BHT for baht.

  • scribe_v3_live (3 runs): โปรเจกต์ in Thai script, but Framework and Deploy capitalized mid-sentence, and 26 partials instead of 9. Not declared.

  • Errors are {"message_type":"auth_error","error":"…"} then a close (bad key).

Not covered: noisy audio, real Thai speech with numbers, a session longer than a minute at live pace.

iApp realtime (2026-11-14)¶

iApp’s realtime-v2, wss://api.iapp.co.th/v3/store/speech/speech-to-text/realtime-v2, header apikey (a bad or missing key is an HTTP 401 before the upgrade). The model cannot be chosen; the server’s ready message reports chinda-asr-realtime-v1. Free during the vendor’s launch (their docs). The client sends a JSON settings message (language_code: "th" is the only language, sample_rate, encoding, latency_ms, end_silence_ms); the server answers ready; audio is binary PCM16; the text message end finishes the turn, followed by the remaining finals and done.

On the 9.2 s Thai fixture (about 12 sessions):

  • Text: Thai, with English words written in Thai script (เฟรมเวิร์ค, เอเลเซีย for Elysia, ดีพลอย, มองโก้ดีบีแอดลัส), as the vendor’s docs say; deploy to Vercel came out as ดีพลอยไปเที่ยวเลวเวย์. The same text in the live run and the fast runs at the default settings.

  • English speech is not supported: the synthetic English clips came back as Thai nonsense (ไอฮันตีแอปเอิลส์เตอร์ฟิฟตี…). A 37 s mixed clip came through fine in its Thai parts and mangled in the English ones, with a few Latin words slipping through.

  • Partials: 14 at the default latency_ms (560), the first at 1.1–1.5 s, about every 0.6 s, each replacing the one before. At latency_ms 80 there are 39 (the first at 0.8 s), at 320 24, at 1120 7–14 (the first at 1.2 s).

  • latency_ms changes the text: 80 wrote มังโก้ดีแอดลัส (dropping the บี of MongoDB) in 2 of 2 runs, and 320 เฟรมเวิร์ค/มังโกดีบีแอดลาส; 560 wrote มองโก้ดีบีแอดลัส. So the provider keeps the vendor’s default.

  • End to done: 19–26 ms at live pace; the sentence’s final arrives about 0.45 s before the audio ends (the clip ends with silence longer than end_silence_ms).

  • Faster than real time works, and gives the same text: the 9.2 s clip all at once finished in 1.7 s (0.75 s in the provider), a 37 s clip in 2.5 s.

  • TOO_FAST (more than 120 s of audio is waiting; send audio in real time) ends the session when too much audio is waiting: a 150 s clip sent at once got it 0.5 s in, with no final text. The provider therefore sends up to 90 s at once and the rest at twice real time: 149 s took 30 s and 298 s took 106 s, no error, so the vendor clears that backlog at least twice as fast as it arrives.

  • Concurrency: two sessions at once worked; a third on the same key got HTTP 429.

  • A 0.1 s clip finishes normally with no text.

Not covered: noisy audio, real Thai speech with numbers, end_silence_ms variations, the BUSY limit (20 live sessions at launch, per the docs).

Partials come about every 0.3 s (31 on the 9.2 s clip). The stream gives transcript-delta (finalized text) and transcript-partial (the provisional text after the latest delta), then one transcript-final with the whole transcript once the input ends.

For silence, the SDK’s text promise rejects with “No transcript generated” even though fullStream ended cleanly with an empty final.

After the stream finishes, the SDK closes the WebSocket with code 1000, but its TLS socket stays open for about 10 s more before it closes. A short CLI run therefore exits about 10 s after printing its transcript.

$0.54 per hour of audio (the gateway’s listed price).

Forbidding the behaviour did not work. Adding “never output tags in angle brackets such as <fil>” to the old wording left it at 10/10. Removing the word transcribe did, taking both defects to 0/10 — the working theory being that “transcribe … verbatim” selects a transcription-annotation register where those tags belong, and naming the token without leaving that register may prime it.
